What should you eat for a better night's sleep?

Phil

Hi Georgie.

Hi Phil. Now, Phil, do you ever struggle to get a good night's sleep?

Phil

You know, I do sometimes. If I've been thinking about something a lot, or even worse, if I've had a cup of coffee later than I should, sometimes I can lie there for hours.

Georgie

Yes. Well, many of us will be familiar with that feeling of lying in bed, feeling really tired, but just not being able to go to sleep, but what causes us to struggle to sleep and is there anything we can do about it? Well, researchers have for a while been interested in the link between what we eat and how we sleep. Today on 6 Minute English, we'll be talking about this and asking, "What should you eat for a better night's sleep?" OK. Phil, let's start with a quiz question. a) 15%,

Georgie

Phil

Georgie, you've been looking at the research on this. Do scientists think that what we eat affects how we sleep?

Georgie

Yes, they do. Scientists have done some long-term studies. So, looking at large groups of people over a long period of time, and they found that a healthier diet is associated with better quality sleep. They've also done some smaller studies looking in more detail at exactly what people are eating and then studying how they sleep in a laboratory.

Phil

OK. And in these studies, have they found that particular foods are better for our sleep than others?

Georgie

Well, it's less about particular foods and more about particular nutrients. So, nutrients means important things your body needs to survive and be healthy. For example, protein is a nutrient. BBC World Service programme The Food Chain has been speaking to researcher Professor Marie-Pierre St-Onge about which nutrients are important for sleep.

Marie-Pierre St-Onge (BBC The Food Chain)

"So, diets that look more like a Mediterranean pattern higher in fruits and vegetables, whole grains, lower in animal-based foods, lower in sugar. But then from our own research, where we evaluate food intakes in the lab, we see different... similar patterns, really. But when we talk about nutrients, diets that are higher in fibre, which we find in fruits and vegetables and whole grains, and lower in saturated fats, which we find in more plant-based types of foods, are associated with more deep sleep at night."

Georgie

Marie-Pierre says that Mediterranean-style diets are associated with good sleep. A Mediterranean diet includes lots of vegetables and less animal-based foods such as meat and dairy.

Phil

Yes, and Marie-Pierre's own research shows that some nutrients, such as fibre, are important for sleep and others, such as saturated fats, are less important. Saturated fat is a type of fat mainly found in meat and dairy that's thought to be bad for your health.

Georgie

Right. And according to Marie-Pierre, eating less saturated fat and more nutrients like fibre is associated with more deep sleep at night. A deep sleep is when you are more fully asleep and your body is able to rest.

Phil

So, Georgie, it sounds like thinking about our diets is important if we want to improve our sleep. And now I've heard that certain foods have a lot of nutrients that can be helpful for sleep. Is that true?

Georgie

Well, there is some limited research on specific foods and their impact on sleep. So, for example, scientists have been studying kiwi fruits and also tart cherry juice. And you may also see adverts for supplements that claim to improve sleep. Supplements are tablets you take, which contain particular nutrients that you might not get in your everyday diet. But experts say that focusing on our whole diet, rather than one particular fruit or supplement, is going to be the most effective. Here's sleep doctor Allie Hare explaining more to BBC World Service programme The Food Chain.

Allie Hare (Sleep Doctor, The Food Chain)

"I think people are looking for the one supplement often or the... you know, we're fed a lot of these things, aren't we, on social media? This magical powder or this magical tablet that you can take that will, you know, help you sleep better immediately from tonight. And of course, you know, Marie-Pierre and I know that there is no such magic bullet, unfortunately. And if there was, I'm sure, you know, we would be... we would be doing very well for ourselves."

Phil

Allie says people are looking for a supplement that can help them sleep.

Georgie

Yes. She says we are fed information on social media about magical tablets. If you are fed information, you are given lots of it in a continuous way.

Phil

But Allie argues that experts know there's no magic bullet to help you sleep, and a magic bullet refers to a simple and effective solution to a problem that's usually one that doesn't exist. OK. Well, now I think it's time to find out the answer to your quiz question, Georgie.

IELTS Speaking Part 2

Topic: Health & Sleep Habits

01:00

Describe a healthy lifestyle habit you adopted to improve your sleep or well-being.

You should say:

  • What habit it is and when you started doing it
  • How you found out about it (diet, exercise, or routine)
  • Whether you ever struggled to get a good night's sleep before
  • And explain how this change has affected the quality of your sleep.

Các cụm từ cần lồng ghép để đạt Band 7.0 - 8.0:

# struggle to get a good night's sleep # lie there for hours # associated with better quality sleep # Mediterranean pattern # higher in fibre # lower in saturated fats # deep sleep # no magic bullet

Part 3 Discussion Questions (Thảo luận chuyên sâu):

1. Why do so many people in modern society struggle with sleep deprivation?

Xem gợi ý (thử trả lời trước nhé)

Gợi ý trả lời: Nhắc tới nghiên cứu 35% người trưởng thành ngủ ít hơn 7 tiếng; thói quen dùng caffeine muộn, stress công việc và việc ăn nhiều thực phẩm chế biến sẵn chứa saturated fats.

2. Should consumers rely on sleep supplements advertised on social media?

Xem gợi ý (thử trả lời trước nhé)

Gợi ý trả lời: Nhấn mạnh cảnh báo của Dr Allie Hare: "we are constantly fed information on social media", "there is no magic bullet", giải pháp bền vững nhất là "focus on our whole diet".

Model Answer Band 8.0

"A couple of years ago, I used to struggle to get a good night's sleep. Whenever I drank coffee late or experienced heavy work stress, I would just lie there for hours feeling utterly exhausted yet unable to drift off."

"Everything changed when I learned that dietary choices are directly associated with better quality sleep. Rather than falling for online supplements that claim to be a magic bullet, I decided to focus on my whole diet by shifting towards a Mediterranean pattern."

"I cut down on animal-based foods and processed snacks loaded with saturated fats, and started incorporating abundant whole grains and legumes higher in fibre. Within just three weeks, I noticed a dramatic increase in my restorative deep sleep, waking up refreshed and ready for the day."

Điểm cộng Lexical & Coherence:

• Diễn đạt tự nhiên: Dùng "drift off", "lie there for hours" thay vì lặp từ "cannot sleep".

• Topic-specific collocations: Sử dụng chuẩn xác "Mediterranean pattern", "higher in fibre", "saturated fats", "no magic bullet", "restorative deep sleep".
